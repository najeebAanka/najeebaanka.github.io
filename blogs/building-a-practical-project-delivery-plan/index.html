Build a Delivery Plan Your Team Can Use

By Najeeb Anka

In this article

A project plan earns its place when it helps people decide what to do next. A detailed schedule can still leave the team uncertain if outcomes, responsibilities, or dependencies are unclear. Start with a plan people can discuss and update as they learn.

Describe the outcome in plain language

Write a short description of what should be different when the project is delivered. Identify who will use the result, what they need to accomplish, and what will count as acceptable. Confirm this with the people responsible for the decision before splitting it into tasks.

For a customer portal, an outcome might be that customers can find a particular document and request help. That gives the team something more concrete to plan than “deliver a better experience.”

Break the journey into reviewable milestones

Choose milestones that represent something people can inspect or decide. An agreed requirements outline, a working user journey, and an approved handover are useful examples. “Development in progress” describes activity but does not show what is ready.

For each milestone, record the expected result and who will review it. Leave room for feedback and corrections before work becomes dependent on an unconfirmed assumption.

Identify dependencies before fixing the dates

Ask what must be available before each stage can begin. Content, external accounts, access permissions, design decisions, and stakeholder availability can all affect the sequence. Assign someone to follow up on each dependency.

Discuss uncertain inputs when you agree on timing. A plan that assumes every unanswered question will resolve immediately makes delays difficult to explain and even harder to recover from.

Give work a clear owner

Separate the person responsible for doing the work from the person who can approve it. Several people may contribute, but an identifiable owner helps keep questions moving. Make the route for unresolved decisions clear too.

  • What result is expected?
  • Who is responsible for moving it forward?
  • What does that person need from others?
  • Who can confirm that it is ready?

Review the plan as evidence changes

Use a regular check-in to compare completed work, emerging risks, and decisions needed. Update the plan when new information changes the sequence or effort. Keep the reason for meaningful changes visible so everyone understands the current expectation.

A useful review ends with actions and owners. If a milestone is at risk, agree whether to adjust scope, change the order, add time, or seek another decision.

Plan the handover as part of delivery

Include documentation, access transfer, operational checks, and any training required to use the result. Agree who accepts the handover and what support remains afterwards. These are deliverables with dependencies, not tasks to discover after launch.

A compact planning record: outcome, milestone, owner, dependencies, target date, acceptance criteria, and next review. Keep the level of detail useful to the people doing the work.
