Mobile-First Design: Build Around the Essential Journey

By Najeeb Anka

In this article

Choose the first useful action Let content determine the layout changes Make navigation and controls usable Treat images and motion deliberately Keep important content consistent Review the whole journey on a real phone

A narrow screen makes design decisions visible. Long introductions, crowded navigation, and competing buttons quickly become difficult to use. Starting small helps you identify the essential journey, then expand the layout as more space becomes available.

Choose the first useful action

Decide what visitors need to understand before taking the next step. On a service website, that may be a concise introduction, a relevant project, and a contact action. Put those elements in an order that makes sense without relying on a wide desktop composition.

Keep supporting information accessible rather than removing it simply to shorten the page. Prioritisation should improve the route through the content.

Let content determine the layout changes

Use flexible widths and introduce layout changes when the content becomes cramped. A card grid might move from one column to two when each card has enough room, rather than following a list of named devices. MDN’s responsive design guide covers flexible layouts and media queries.

Test at the widths between your intended layouts too. A page that works at two screenshot sizes can still have problems between them.

Make navigation and controls usable

Use recognisable labels, comfortable spacing between controls, and clear feedback when a menu opens. Make its links reachable by keyboard and return focus sensibly when it closes. A phone layout should not depend on a precise hover or an icon with no accessible name.

For forms, keep labels visible and error messages close to the relevant field. Ask only for information needed at this stage of the conversation.

Treat images and motion deliberately

Check whether a crop still communicates the subject on a small screen. Reserve image space so content does not jump as files load. Avoid an oversized decorative visual that pushes the main explanation far down the page.

Keep animations short and avoid taking control of scrolling. Essential content should remain available when motion is reduced or JavaScript does not run.

Keep important content consistent

Google’s mobile-first indexing guidance explains that it uses the mobile version of content for indexing and ranking. Keep meaningful content and metadata consistent across screen sizes rather than treating the phone experience as an incomplete version.

This does not make a responsive layout a promise of better rankings. It is a reason to make the smaller-screen experience complete and understandable.

Review the whole journey on a real phone

Follow a visitor’s route from arrival through the final contact action. Try longer text, browser zoom, a slower connection, and both portrait and landscape orientation. Check that sticky elements do not cover buttons or reading content.

A useful review: can someone understand the offer, find relevant evidence, and reach you comfortably with one hand? Fix the interruptions in that journey before adding visual extras.
